# Working Capital Efficiency

## Business Problem

Efficient working capital management is critical for maintaining liquidity and improving operational performance. However, many firms struggle to identify whether receivables or payables have a greater influence on the Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC).

This project evaluates the relative impact of Accounts Receivable (AR) Days and Accounts Payable (AP) Days on working capital efficiency across Indian FMCG companies.

---

## Objective

To determine whether managing receivables or payables provides greater control over a firm's Cash Conversion Cycle.

---

## Dataset

- 226 Indian FMCG companies
- Period: 2011–2024
- Panel dataset

---

## Methodology

- Panel Data Analysis
- Fixed Effects Model
- Random Effects Model
- Hausman Test
- Model Diagnostics

---

## Tools Used

- EViews
- Microsoft Excel

---

## Key Findings

- Receivable Days have the strongest impact on Cash Conversion Cycle.
- A nearly one-to-one relationship exists between receivable days and CCC.
- Payable Days influence liquidity but contribute less than receivables.
- Firm-specific characteristics significantly affect working capital efficiency.

---

## Business Impact

The findings suggest that companies should prioritize faster receivable collection before attempting to extend supplier payment periods.

Improving collection efficiency can significantly strengthen liquidity without increasing external financing.
